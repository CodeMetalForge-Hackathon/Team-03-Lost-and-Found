The '''Leonard P. Zakim Bunker Hill Memorial Bridge''' ({{IPAc-en|ˈ|z|eɪ|k|ə|m}}; also known as '''"The Zakim"''') is a [[cable-stayed bridge]] completed in 2003 across the [[Charles River]] in [[Boston]], [[Massachusetts]]. It replaced the [[Charlestown High Bridge]], an older [[truss bridge]] constructed in the 1950s.

The bridge and connecting tunnel were built as part of the [[Big Dig]], the largest [[highway]] construction project in the United States. The bridge's unique styling quickly became an [[Secular icon|icon]] for Boston, often featured in the backdrop of national news channels to establish location, and included on tourist souvenirs. The bridge is commonly referred to as the "'''Zakim Bridge'''" or "'''Bunker Hill Bridge'''" by residents of nearby [[Charlestown, Boston|Charlestown]].

The [[Leverett Circle Connector Bridge]] was constructed in conjunction with the Zakim Bridge, allowing some traffic to bypass it.

== Design ==

The bridge concept was designed by [[Switzerland|Swiss]] [[civil engineer]] [[Christian Menn]] in collaboration with bridge designer [[Miguel Rosales]] and its design was engineered by American civil engineer Ruchu Hsu with Parsons Brinckerhoff (now [[WSP USA]]).<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://beaconhilltimes.com/2012/04/11/miguel-rosales-building-a-better-bridge/|title=Miguel Rosales: Building a better bridge – Beacon Hill Times|website=beaconhilltimes.com|access-date=2020-02-06|language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://culturenow.org/entry%26permalink%3D05598%26seo%3DLeonard-Zakim-Bunker-Hill-Bridge_Miguel-Rosales-HNTB-Christian--Menn-Theodore-Zoli-Richard-Hsu-and-Parsons-Brinckerhoff|title=CultureNOW - Leonard Zakim Bunker Hill Bridge: Miguel Rosales, HNTB, Christian Menn, Theodore Zoli, Richard Hsu and Parsons Brinckerhoff|website=culturenow.org|access-date=2020-02-06|archive-date=January 17, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230117064931/https://culturenow.org/entry%26permalink%3D05598%26seo%3DLeonard-Zakim-Bunker-Hill-Bridge_Miguel-Rosales-HNTB-Christian--Menn-Theodore-Zoli-Richard-Hsu-and-Parsons-Brinckerhoff|url-status=dead|language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.bostonglobe.com/metro/2013/05/30/for-lawrence-students-zakim-bridge-project-leads-acclaim-personal-tour-span-architect/eJJ6lEqmEt77yAvI0RU6RN/story.html|title=For Lawrence students, Zakim Bridge project leads to acclaim, personal tour by span's architect - The Boston Globe|last=Sullivan|first=James|website=BostonGlobe.com|access-date=2020-02-06|language=en}}</ref> Wallace Floyd Associates, sub-consultants to Bechtel/Parsons Brinckerhoff, was the lead architect/urban designer and facilitated community participation during the design process.<ref>Thomas P. Hughes, ''Rescuing Prometheus'', New York, Pantheon, 1998</ref><ref>Wiley Online Library June 19, 2013, ''Megaproject Management" Lessons in Risk and Project Management from the Big Dig'', pp. 415–418</ref>

The bridge is a [[cable-stayed bridge]] in a [[Cable-stayed bridge#Designs|harp configuration]] with [[Cable-stayed bridge#Cable-stayed cradle-system bridge|cradles]] carrying each strand through its pylon. The top of each pylon is shaped like an [[obelisk]], echoing the nearby [[Bunker Hill Monument]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.upi.com/Archives/2001/01/17/New-Charles-River-bridge-dramatic-controversial/9183979707600/|date=January 17, 2001|website=[[United Press International|UPI]]|last=Haskell|first=David D.|title= New Charles River bridge dramatic, controversial|accessdate=July 5, 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bostonmagazine.com/2010/08/04/why-i-love-boston-americas-bridge/|last=Diamant|first=Anita|title= Why I Love Boston: America’s Bridge|website=[[Boston Magazine]]|date=August 4, 2010|accessdate=July 5, 2026}}</ref>

The main portion of the Zakim Bridge carries four lanes each way (northbound and southbound) of [[Interstate 93]] ([[Concurrency (road)|concurrent]] with [[U.S. Route 1 in Massachusetts|U.S. Route 1]]) between the [[Thomas P. O'Neill Jr. Tunnel|Thomas P. "Tip" O'Neill Jr. Tunnel]] and the elevated highway to the north. Two additional lanes are [[cantilevered]] outside the cables, which carry northbound traffic from the [[Sumner Tunnel]] and [[North End, Boston|North End]] on-ramp. These lanes merge with the main highway north of the bridge. I-93 heads toward [[New Hampshire]] as the "Northern Expressway", and US 1 splits from the [[Interstate Highway System|Interstate]] and travels northeast toward Massachusetts' [[North Shore (Massachusetts)|North Shore]] communities, crossing the [[Mystic River]] via the [[Tobin Bridge]].

The 1975-built [[MBTA Orange Line#Rerouting of Charlestown and Everett service|MBTA Orange Line's Haymarket North Extension]] tunnel lies beneath the bridge.

== Dedication ==
The bridge's full name commemorates Boston area leader and civil rights activist [[Leonard P. Zakim]], who championed "building bridges between peoples",<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.masspike.com/user-cgi/news.cgi?dbkey=54&type=Archived&src=newsarchive |title=Leonard P. Zakim Bunker Hill Bridge Dedication Events Set For October 3–6 |author=MTA press release |date=September 18, 2002 |access-date=April 28, 2008 |quote="He worked tirelessly to build personal bridges between our city's diverse people and neighborhoods." - Joyce Zakim, wife of Lenny Zakim |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070210223527/http://www.masspike.com/user-cgi/news.cgi?dbkey=54&type=Archived&src=newsarchive |archive-date=February 10, 2007}}</ref> and the [[Battle of Bunker Hill]].

The bridge was dedicated on October 4, 2002, in a ceremony held on the new span. The dedication speakers included members of Zakim's family, government officials, and a performance of the song "[[Thunder Road (song)|Thunder Road]]" by [[Bruce Springsteen]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://wncx.cbslocal.com/photo-galleries/2011/09/23/born-to-run-bruce-springsteen-turns-62-photos/bruce-springsteen-performs-at-the-leonar-p-zakim-bunker-hill-bridge-dedication/ |title=Bruce Springsteen performs at the Leonar (sic) P. Zakim Bunker Hill Bridge Dedication |access-date=May 20, 2015 |archive-date=November 10, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171110061836/http://wncx.cbslocal.com/photo-galleries/2011/09/23/born-to-run-bruce-springsteen-turns-62-photos/bruce-springsteen-performs-at-the-leonar-p-zakim-bunker-hill-bridge-dedication/ |url-status=dead }}</ref>

Introducing the song, Springsteen said about Zakim, "... I knew him a little bit during the last year of his life, he was one of those people whose, intensity, inner spirit you could feel even when he was very ill, ... we honor his memory obviously not with this beautiful bridge, very lovely, but by continuing on in his fight for social justice."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://brucebase.wikispaces.com/2002-10-04+-+LEONARD+P.+ZAKIM+BUNKER+HILL+MEMORIAL+BRIDGE,+BOSTON,+MA |title=Brucebase transcription |access-date=May 20, 2015 |archive-date=December 28, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111228094614/http://brucebase.wikispaces.com/2002-10-04+-+LEONARD+P.+ZAKIM+BUNKER+HILL+MEMORIAL+BRIDGE%2C+BOSTON%2C+MA |url-status=dead }}</ref>

== Landscape design and public art ==
{{multiple image|align=horizontal
|image1=Five Beacons for the Lost Half Mile blue phase.jpg
|caption1=Under Zakim Bridge, Five Beacons for the Lost Half Mile, blue phase
|image2=Five Beacons for the Lost Half Mile orange phase.jpg
|caption2=Five Beacons for the Lost Half Mile, orange phase, View to West
}}

Placement of footings for the Zakim Bridge required environmental permits to relocate areas of open water surface, changing the contour of the [[Charles River]] shoreline. The process of landscape design and environmental mitigation under the bridge deck and around the bridge supports allowed for the creation of a new and accessible public landscape designed by Carol R. Johnson Associates. This under-bridge landscape contains a series of perforated stainless steel lighting-based public artworks, entitled ''Five Beacons for the Lost Half Mile.''

Pedestrians and cyclists are able to travel from Charlestown toward Cambridge over the adjacent [[North Bank Bridge]] to [[North Point Park (Massachusetts)|North Point Park]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.thecharles.org/documents/northbank.pdf |title=Twists & Turns Geometric constraints posed a major challenge for designers of a new footbridge |access-date=July 26, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120318115041/http://www.thecharles.org/documents/northbank.pdf |archive-date=March 18, 2012 |url-status=dead }}</ref> This bridge is a link in the [[Charles River Bike Path]] and a segment of the [[Mass Central Rail Trail#Mass Central Rail Trail - Waltham to Boston|Mass Central Rail Trail]] and the [[East Coast Greenway]].
