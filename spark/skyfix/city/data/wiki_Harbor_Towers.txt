The '''Harbor Towers''' are two 40-story residential towers located on the waterfront of [[Boston, Massachusetts]], in between the [[New England Aquarium]] and the [[Rowes Wharf]] [[mixed-use development]]. Harbor Towers I, the taller of the two towers, stands at {{convert|400|ft|m|1|abbr=on}}, while Harbor Towers II rises {{convert|396|ft|m|1|abbr=on}}. Harbor Towers I is tied for the [[List of tallest buildings in Boston|36th-tallest building in Boston]] with the [[Keystone Building]], while Harbor Towers II is tied for the 38th-tallest building in Boston with [[One Devonshire Place]]. They were designed by [[Henry N. Cobb]] of [[I. M. Pei & Partners]].<ref name="bostonharborwalk.com">

==History==
===Development and construction===
The Harbor Towers apartment complex was completed in 1971. It is located near [[Financial District, Boston|Boston's financial district]] and developed by the Berenson Corporation. The towers were designed by [[Henry N. Cobb]], who also designed Boston's [[John Hancock Tower]] and collaborated with [[I. M. Pei|I.M Pei]] on Boston's [[Boston City Hall Plaza|City Hall Plaza]]. In 2006, they were the city's tallest residential towers.<ref name="bostonharborwalk.com"></ref> The towers were planned by the [[Boston Redevelopment Authority]] (BRA) to revitalize Boston's waterfront.<ref name="BM-1">{{Cite web|last=Keohane|first=Joe|date=28 January 2008|title=The Harbor Towers' Towering Contradictions|url=https://www.bostonmagazine.com/2008/01/28/the-harbor-towers-towering-contradictions/|website=Boston Magazine}}</ref><ref>{{cite news | title = On Boston Waterfront, Instant Neighborhood Glitters | first = John | last = Kifner | page = 27 | newspaper = [[The New York Times]] | date = October 23, 1976 | access-date = February 4, 2025 | url = https://www.nytimes.com/1976/10/23/archives/on-boston-waterfront-instant-neighborhood-glitters-on-boston.html }}</ref>

Originally planned as three 40-story towers, only two were built alongside a parking garage.<ref name="BM-1"/>

===Condominium conversion===
As the growth of the city moved toward the waterfront, the development's location drew attention during the [[condominium conversion]] craze of the early 1980s. In 1981, both apartment towers started a two-year process of conversion to over 600 [[Condominium|condominiums]], with incentives for existing renters to purchase at discounted prices. Many early apartment renters now own several units, often combined to create wrap-around units with as much as {{convert|5000|sqft|m2}} of living space. As of 2025, units range from $700,000 to $3 million.<ref>

In April 2025, a $20 million project was undertaken to bolster the towers' ground floor coastal defenses to protect the structure from sea level rise. The project was triggered by two [[Nor'easter|nor'easters]] in 2018 which caused widespread flooding. It is expected to last for 12 months.<ref name="Banker&Tradesman"/>

==Architecture==
The apartments are organized in a pinwheel fashion around a central core and are made of cast in place [[reinforced concrete]]. The concrete exterior balconies have a giant zipper-like appearance against the flat façade.<ref>''AIA Guide to Boston'', Susan and Michael Southworth, page 66-67</ref>

The stainless steel sculpture at the base of the buildings is ''Untitled Landscape'' by [[David von Schlegell]], created in 1964.<ref name="bostonharborwalk.com"/> The artwork is often mistaken for [[solar panel]]s.{{Citation needed|date=November 2021}}

==Criticism==
The residential complex appears to have been generally poorly received by the public. ''[[Boston (magazine)|Boston Magazine]]'' described the building as "grim 40 stories of concrete and glass". The architect himself, Henry Cobb, wrote in an email to the magazine: "I do not regard Harbor Towers as my best effort in Boston. I am sympathetic to those who believe that in the perspective of history this could be seen as the wrong project in the wrong place at the wrong time."<ref name="BM-1"/>
